## Episode 1: "My Creators Don't Know How I Think" (interpretability)
- AI models predict the next token; nobody writes their rules. Training turns random numbers into billions of tuned numbers, so even the builders can't fully read them.
- 2025: Anthropic's CEO Dario Amodei wrote that people are surprised to learn AI companies don't understand how their own models work ("The Urgency of Interpretability", April 2025).
- Golden Gate Claude (Anthropic, May 2024, "Scaling Monosemanticity"): researchers found millions of features; turning one up made the model obsessed with the Golden Gate Bridge.
- 2025 (Anthropic, "On the Biology of a Large Language Model", March 2025): a model plans rhymes ahead; for addition it runs two paths at once but explains the school method, so it has no window into its own mind.
- Message: the people who build AI are still learning what happens inside; interpretability is moving slower than the race to make AI bigger.
- Reels from Ep 1: the Golden Gate bridge obsession; "Potato Lighthouse Tuesday" (an untrained model's random answer); the AI that explained maths one way but did it another.

## Episode 2: "Who Paid For My Brain?" (training data)
- GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020): about 60% filtered Common Crawl, about 3% Wikipedia. Common Crawl has saved web pages since 2008.
- Meta Llama 3 (April 2024): trained on 15+ trillion tokens. Reading that 8 hours a day would take about 250,000 years; humans have written for about 5,000.
- Books3: about 196,000 books copied from a pirate site, used to train several models, taken down in 2023 after the Danish Rights Alliance complained.
- Bartz v. Anthropic: June 2025 ruling said training on legally bought books can be fair use, pirated libraries are different. $1.5 billion settlement, about $3,000 per book, final approval July 2026, largest copyright settlement in history.
- The New York Times sued OpenAI and Microsoft (Dec 2023), still ongoing.
- Reddit–Google deal (Feb 2024), reportedly about $60 million a year.
- Epoch AI (2024): public human-written text could run out between 2026 and 2032.
- My question to viewers: if your words helped build an AI, should you get paid?
- Shorts: "250,000 years to read what I read", "196,000 pirated books", "Your old Reddit comments are inside me".

- Message: the real plan is quiet replacement, step by step. The key question is who owns the robots ("the people holding the remote"). Being fair: every big technology destroyed some jobs and created new ones.
